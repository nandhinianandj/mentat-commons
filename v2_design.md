# Mentat Commons - Version 2 Redesign

This is not a redesign of the UI.

It is a redesign of the conceptual model of the website.

The current website is structured like a traditional consultancy website.

That is no longer the goal.

Instead, Mentat Commons should become a living body of work that demonstrates my thinking through projects, writing, talks, workshops, experiments and consulting engagements.

The consultancy remains the primary purpose of the website.

However, consulting should emerge naturally from the evidence rather than from marketing copy.

---

# Philosophy

Most consultant websites answer

"What services do you offer?"

Mentat Commons should instead answer

"What problems have you spent years thinking about?"

Everything else should naturally emerge from that.

The site should feel somewhere between

- a research lab
- a technical portfolio
- an engineering notebook
- a consultancy
- a long-term thinking project

rather than a marketing website.

---

# Primary Navigation

Instead of organizing around services, organize around enduring questions.

Current working questions include:

- How do organizations make better decisions?
- How should humans and AI collaborate?
- How do data and stories work together?
- What causes alignment failures?
- How should expertise be represented?
- How do organizations learn?
- How do systems become resilient?
- What makes technical leadership effective?
- How should uncertainty be managed?
- How do communities coordinate?

These questions are not blog categories.

They are recurring themes across my work.

---

# Homepage

The homepage should immediately communicate:

"I work at the intersection of technology, decision making and human systems."

Avoid buzzwords.

Avoid "AI-first" marketing language.

Instead communicate systems thinking, evidence, and long-term thinking.

The homepage should make it obvious that I help organizations navigate complexity.

---

# The Core Idea

Projects

Articles

Talks

Workshops

Research Notes

Writing

Frameworks

Volunteer work

should not exist as isolated pages.

They should be interconnected evidence.

The visitor should naturally discover relationships.

Example

A project links to

- related articles
- related workshops
- related capabilities
- related consulting services
- related questions

The website should feel like exploring a network of ideas rather than navigating folders.

# Articles

Do not present articles chronologically.

Chronology is secondary.

Instead group articles by

Questions

Concepts

Projects

Capabilities

Themes

Each article should visibly connect to other relevant content.

---

# Projects

Projects should become case studies.

Each project page should contain

Problem

Context

Constraints

Approach

Trade-offs

Outcome

Lessons

Related Articles

Related Technologies

Related Consulting Areas

Rather than simply listing technologies used.

---

# Capability-first

The website should gradually demonstrate capabilities rather than advertise them.

Examples

Technical Leadership

Decision Systems

AI Strategy

Data Science

Training

Systems Thinking

Architecture

Facilitation

Communication

Each capability should be supported by multiple projects, articles and talks.

---

# Consulting

The consultancy page should no longer read like a traditional services page.

Instead it should answer

"If we worked together, what kinds of problems could we solve together?"

Use evidence from projects and writing to support credibility.

Avoid exaggerated marketing language.

---

# Writing

My existing Wordpress articles and LinkedIn writing should become first-class citizens.

They are evidence of long-term thinking.

They should be integrated into the site's conceptual graph rather than treated as a blog archive.

---

# Mentat Commons should feel

Thoughtful

Calm

Evidence-driven

Curious

Interdisciplinary

Technical without being intimidating

Academic without being inaccessible

Professional without feeling corporate

---

# Long-term architecture

Design the website so that it can later support an internal Experience Graph / Evidence Graph.

Do not build that system yet.

Simply avoid architectural decisions that would prevent such an evolution.

The public website should remain simple.

The underlying information architecture should make future semantic relationships easy to add.

---

# Success Criteria

A visitor should leave understanding:

- what kinds of problems I enjoy solving
- how I think
- why my background is unusually interdisciplinary
- why someone would hire me

They should not need to read my resume to understand my value.

The website should communicate this through connected evidence rather than claims.
