Decision & Learning Labs

Practice-based training for better judgment, coordination, and learning

Decision & Learning Labs are the core training format at Mentat Commons.

They are designed for organizations that want learning to change:

how decisions are made,

how teams coordinate under uncertainty,

and how insight is carried forward into future work.

Labs are not lectures, simulations-for-fun, or generic workshops.
They are structured environments for practicing real decisions.

What a “lab” means in practice

A lab is a bounded space where teams:

work with real situations from their context,

slow down decision-making at critical moments,

examine how data, narratives, and incentives interact,

and reflect on outcomes before returning to execution.

Each lab is designed around process clarity, not performance theater.

The core learning loop

Every Decision & Learning Lab follows the same loop:

Situation framing
A real decision, tension, or recurring challenge from the organization.

Evidence and constraints
Data, signals, incentives, timelines, and uncertainties that shape the situation.

Narrative construction
How participants explain what is happening and what matters.

Decision or action
A choice is made — explicitly, with ownership.

Reflection and revision
Assumptions are surfaced, outcomes examined, alternatives considered.

Learning happens between these steps, not inside any single one.

What labs are used for

Decision & Learning Labs are commonly used to:

improve decision quality in complex or uncertain environments,

integrate data and analysis into real workflows,

reduce friction between technical and non-technical roles,

practice high-stakes decisions before consequences are real,

surface and negotiate trade-offs early,

build shared language across teams.

They are especially effective where “more information” has not led to better outcomes.

Lab focus areas (adapted per organization)

Labs may emphasize different aspects depending on need:

Decision & Data Practice

Reasoning with incomplete or noisy data

Interpreting models and analytics critically

Making uncertainty explicit

Explaining decisions clearly to others

Applied Systems Thinking

Connecting decisions to system behavior

Understanding feedback loops and incentives

Identifying where structure drives outcomes

Collaboration & Coordination

Cross-functional decision-making

Negotiation and trade-offs

Handling disagreement productively

Clarifying decision ownership

These are not separate programs — they are different lenses on the same process.

How labs are structured

A typical lab engagement includes:

Pre-work
Short conversations or materials to identify the decision context and constraints.

Live sessions
Facilitated, time-bounded sessions focused on practice, not presentation.

Artifacts
Decision maps, narratives, system sketches, or reflection notes created during the lab.

Synthesis
A short summary capturing insights, risks, and implications for future work.

Labs can be:

single-session (for focused decisions),

multi-session (for ongoing capability building),

or embedded within longer programs.

What makes this different from standard training

Decisions come before content

Process is examined, not assumed

Data and story are treated as complementary

Disagreement is used as information

Reflection is built into the design

The aim is not consensus or performance, but workable clarity.

Outcomes organizations typically see

After Decision & Learning Labs, organizations often report:

faster alignment on decisions,

fewer stalled or circular discussions,

improved trust in how decisions are made,

better use of data without over-reliance,

learning that carries into future work.

These changes persist because teams practice how they think, not just what they know.

Who this is for

Decision & Learning Labs are well suited for:

leadership teams,

cross-functional groups,

research and policy-adjacent teams,

organizations navigating change or growth,

teams dealing with recurring coordination failures.

No prior technical or facilitation background is required.

How to start

Most lab engagements begin with a simple framing question:

“Which decision keeps recurring — and never quite improves?”

That decision becomes the anchor for the lab.

Relationship to consulting and systems work

Labs often:

precede systems or AI/ML consulting,

support the adoption of new systems,

or help teams reflect on systems already in place.

They are a bridge between design and practice.
