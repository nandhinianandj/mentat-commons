/axis-ii/theatre — Theatre & Facilitation for Organizations
Why theatre-based methods work

Many organizational challenges cannot be solved through discussion alone.

Examples:

persistent conflict,

unclear ownership,

misaligned incentives,

unspoken assumptions,

decisions avoided rather than made.

Theatre-based methods make these dynamics visible and workable.

What this offering actually does

This work uses:

facilitation,

applied theatre,

and improvisational methods,

to help teams practice coordination and decision-making, not perform.

There is no acting background required.

Core Facilitation Engagements
1. Decision Rehearsal Workshops

Purpose: practice high-stakes decisions before they happen.

Participants:

step into different roles,

explore consequences of choices,

test assumptions safely.

Outcome:
Better preparation, clearer decision ownership, reduced downstream conflict.

2. Collaboration & Coordination Labs

Purpose: improve how teams work together under uncertainty.

Focus areas include:

cross-functional alignment,

negotiation and trade-offs,

handling disagreement productively,

building trust without forced consensus.

Outcome:
Stronger coordination and fewer breakdowns during execution.

3. Conflict & Dialogue Facilitation

Purpose: address issues that stall progress but remain unspoken.

This work:

surfaces underlying tensions,

reframes conflict as information,

supports negotiated commitments rather than artificial harmony.

Outcome:
Increased psychological safety and clearer paths forward.

How sessions are structured

Grounded in real organizational challenges

Time-bounded and facilitated

Focused on insight → action → reflection

Explicit about goals and limits

This is not open-ended group therapy or entertainment.

Who this is for

Teams navigating change or growth

Organizations facing repeated coordination failures

Leadership groups dealing with disagreement or uncertainty

Institutions investing in culture as a strategic asset

What changes when this works

Organizations typically see:

improved coordination under pressure,

fewer avoided decisions,

more productive disagreement,

learning that carries forward into daily work.

How to start

Most engagements begin with one question:

“Where are we avoiding practice — and paying the cost later?”

From there, we design a focused intervention rather than a generic program.
