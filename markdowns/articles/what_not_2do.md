*(A List of What Breaks Us)*

Do not turn law into theater.

When justice disappears,  
law will grow louder.  
More uniforms.  
More slogans.  
More spectacle.

Order will replace care.  
Procedure will replace repair.  
Power will walk free  
while bodies are disciplined.

Do not call this safety.  
It is containment.

—

Do not ask belief to carry what systems refuse to hold.

When agency is stripped,  
meaning rushes in.

Suffering becomes destiny.  
Hierarchy becomes natural.  
Collapse becomes necessary.  

Gods are recruited.  
Ideologies sharpen.  

Not to save people—  
but to make endurance bearable.

Belief hardens  
when reality offers no exits.

—

Do not let education forget how to ask why.

When learning becomes obedience,  
facts turn into badges,  
science into authority,  
doubt into betrayal.

Inquiry becomes disloyal.  
Correction feels like violence.

This is not ignorance.  
It is defense.

—

Do not replace causality with story.

When distance grows,  
myths move in.

Bad actors.  
Bad cultures.  
Bad people.

Blame settles downward.  
Structure stays innocent.

Violence is explained  
instead of traced.

This is how harm  
loses its fingerprints.

—

Do not reward moral paralysis.

Good people will hesitate.  
They will weigh.  
They will fear being wrong.

And in that delay,  
nothing will happen.

Those without reputational risk  
will act freely.

Caution will look like virtue.  
Inaction will pass as care.

—

Do not distribute agency upward  
and guilt downward.

Some are granted intention.  
Others are assigned consequence.

Advice replaces help.  
Empathy replaces solidarity.

The message repeats:
*You are free.*  
*Therefore this is your fault.*

This is how systems teach people  
to police themselves.

—

Do not normalize asymmetry of time.

Certainty for me.  
Uncertainty for thee.

Stability for me.  
Volatility for thee.

Predictability for me.  
Chaos for thee.

Some may fail safely.  
Others cannot fail at all.

Call this opportunity  
if you need the lie.

—

Do not believe myths die when disproven.

They die only  
when they stop being useful.

Beliefs are shelters  
before they are errors.

Debunking without replacement  
is demolition.

People cling harder  
when you pull away the roof.

—

Do not rush to fix belief.

Ask what it is holding.  
Ask what would collapse without it.  
Ask who benefits from its weight.

When systems fail,  
belief does not dissolve.

It calcifies.

And when it finally breaks,  
it breaks violently.

—

This is not an argument  
against law,  
or faith,  
or knowledge.

It is a warning  
about what we force them to become  
when justice goes missing.
