A forest is not trees.  
[pause: 1 breath]  

A forest is a conversation.  
[pause: 2 breaths]  

Root to root.  
[pause: 1 breath]  

Fungal thread.  
[pause: 1 breath]  

Sugar traded for warning.  
[pause: 1 breath]  

Water traded for shade.  
[pause: 2 breaths]  

A message passing underground:  
*not mine.*  
*ours.*  
[pause: 3 breaths]  

One tree falls—  
and the others don’t just watch.  
[pause: 2 breaths]  

They reroute.  
They re-balance.  
They keep the living moving.  
[pause: 4 breaths]  

We call it “nature.”  
[pause: 1 breath]  

But it’s governance—  
without speeches.  
[pause: 5 breaths]

## Interlude I-B: Rhizome Logic

The myth is:  
every tree stands alone.  
[pause: 2 breaths]  

But forests don’t run on independence.  
[pause: 2 breaths]  

They run on networks.  
[pause: 2 breaths]  

Mycelium doesn’t ask  
who deserves help.  
[pause: 2 breaths]  

It asks:  
will the system survive?  
[pause: 3 breaths]  

A rhizome doesn’t “own” a path.  
It spreads one.  
[pause: 3 breaths]  

A fern doesn’t hoard sunlight.  
It learns the angle.  
[pause: 3 breaths]  

That’s what we forgot.  
[pause: 2 breaths]  

Not morality.  
Not kindness.  
[pause: 2 breaths]  

Interdependence as default.  
[pause: 5 breaths]

## Interlude I-C: One Species (Not One Body)

Sometimes I think  
we misunderstand what “one” means.  
[pause: 2 breaths]  

Not sameness.  
Not unity as obedience.  
[pause: 2 breaths]  

One, like a forest—  
multiple lives  
held inside one breathing system.  
[pause: 3 breaths]  

One, like mycelium—  
invisible labor  
turning death into food.  
[pause: 3 breaths]  

One, like roots—  
where the boundary between you and me  
stops being clean.  
[pause: 4 breaths]  

And maybe this is why  
vulnerability feels so expensive.  
[pause: 2 breaths]  

Because we’ve been taught  
to live like single trees  
in a world that only survives  
as a forest.  
[pause: 6 breaths]
