# Rethinking the Business — Part VI

# MLOps Isn't About Models

When people hear MLOps, they usually think about models.

Deployment.

Monitoring.

Inference.

Versioning.

Drift detection.

Infrastructure.

Those are certainly part of it.

But I think they hide a much more interesting question.

**How does an organization know that its understanding of reality is becoming obsolete?**

Concept drift.

Data drift.

Pipeline failures.

Distribution shifts.

Missing data.

Hallucinations.

Evaluation metrics.

At first glance these look like engineering problems.

I don't think they are.

They're symptoms of something deeper.

Organizations continuously build hypotheses about customers.

MLOps exists because those hypotheses inevitably become stale.

A model drifting isn't simply an engineering inconvenience.

It's evidence that reality may have changed.

The same is true for businesses.

Customer habits drift.

Competitors redefine expectations.

Markets evolve.

Culture shifts.

Technology changes behaviour.

The real purpose of monitoring isn't maintaining software.

It's maintaining alignment between an organization's beliefs and reality.

Perhaps MLOps is simply one modern expression of a much older organizational challenge:

**How do we keep learning after we've become successful?**

Technology changes.

That question doesn't.
