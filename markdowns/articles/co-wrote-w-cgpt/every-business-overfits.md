# Rethinking the Business — Part V

# Every Business Overfits

One of the first lessons in machine learning is deceptively simple.

A model can become so good at explaining yesterday's data that it becomes worse at predicting tomorrow's.

We call this **overfitting**.

I've started wondering if businesses suffer from exactly the same disease.

Every successful company is built around a model of customer behaviour.

Who buys.

Why they buy.

How much they'll pay.

How often they'll return.

What influences their decisions.

When that understanding is correct, the business grows.

Processes are documented.

KPIs stabilize.

Departments specialize.

The organization scales.

Ironically, these are the very things that make future adaptation harder.

Processes become policy.

Policy becomes culture.

Culture becomes identity.

Questioning assumptions becomes increasingly expensive.

A company can become extraordinarily efficient...

...at serving customers who no longer exist.

Machine learning has a beautiful idea called **regularization**.

Don't build the most accurate model for yesterday.

Build the most adaptable model for tomorrow.

Businesses need regularization too.

Not in software.

In incentives.

In leadership.

In hiring.

In experimentation.

The organizations that survive aren't those with the most accurate model.

They're the ones that never become too emotionally attached to it.

Because reality has no obligation to remain compatible with yesterday's assumptions.
