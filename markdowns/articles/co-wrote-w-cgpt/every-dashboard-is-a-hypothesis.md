# Every Dashboard Contains a Hypothesis

Dashboards feel objective.

Rows.

Charts.

KPIs.

Green arrows.

Red arrows.

Numbers give us confidence.

But here's the uncomfortable thought.

**A dashboard isn't reality.**

It's an opinion about reality.

Every dashboard answers a question before you've even asked it.

It says:

These are the variables that matter.

These are the relationships worth watching.

These are the signals we believe predict success.

In other words...

Every dashboard contains hypotheses.

Revenue assumes value creation.

NPS assumes loyalty predicts growth.

Daily Active Users assume engagement matters.

Conversion assumes intent.

Churn assumes retention is the key problem.

None of these are universally true.

They're useful models.

Until they aren't.

One of the challenges of growing organizations is that dashboards slowly become institutionalized.

People stop asking why the metrics exist.

They simply optimize them.

Eventually, the metric becomes the mission.

Economists know this as Goodhart's Law:

> When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure.

Machine learning offers another perspective.

We would never trust a model forever without validating that it still reflects reality.

Perhaps dashboards deserve the same treatment.

Instead of asking:

*"Did this KPI improve?"*

We might first ask:

*"Does this KPI still represent what we think it represents?"*

That's a much harder question.

It's also a much more valuable one.
