# Rethinking the Business — Part VII

# Cash Flow is More Than Money

Most conversations about profit revolve around one question.

How should we distribute it?

Shareholders.

Bonuses.

Dividends.

Expansion.

Debt reduction.

Acquisitions.

Important questions.

But I've started wondering if they're preceded by a more interesting one.

**What are we buying with retained earnings?**

If a business is fundamentally a learning system, then profit is more than financial capital.

It's learning capital.

Some of it should protect today's understanding of customers.

Reliable operations.

Existing products.

Customer support.

Infrastructure.

Those are the foundations that keep the current model working.

But another portion should deliberately challenge that model.

Not by chasing every trend.

By attacking assumptions.

What if our customers have changed?

What if our biggest competitor isn't in our industry?

What if our pricing assumptions are obsolete?

What if AI changes how customers discover products?

Nassim Nicholas Taleb's Barbell Strategy offers an interesting lens here.

Be conservative where failure is catastrophic.

Be experimental where upside is asymmetric.

Perhaps organizations should think about their cash flow the same way.

Protect the engine that pays today's bills.

But reserve meaningful resources for discovering why tomorrow's customers may behave differently.

Not because experimentation is fashionable.

Because certainty is expensive.
