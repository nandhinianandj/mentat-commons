# Businesses Don't Run on Data

*"Organizations don't run on data. They run on hypotheses about reality."*

Data has become one of those words that everyone agrees is important.

We're told to become data-driven.

To trust the data.

To let the numbers speak.

But I've started wondering whether that's actually true.

Because data, by itself, doesn't make decisions.

People do.

Organizations do.

And those decisions are rarely based on raw data.

They're based on interpretations of data.

They're based on beliefs.

They're based on hypotheses.

Every KPI says:

> "This matters."

Every dashboard says:

> "These numbers explain reality."

Every quarterly report says:

> "These trends deserve attention."

None of those are objective truths.

They're hypotheses.

A sales dashboard assumes revenue is an important signal.

A customer success dashboard assumes retention predicts long-term health.

A marketing dashboard assumes CAC and conversion rates capture acquisition efficiency.

Someone, somewhere, decided these were the variables worth measuring.

Someone chose what not to measure.

That choice is itself a hypothesis.

This matters because businesses don't fail only when the data is wrong.

They fail when the underlying hypotheses become obsolete.

Customer behaviour changes.

Technology changes.

Regulations change.

Competitors change expectations.

The dashboard still refreshes every morning.

The numbers still look accurate.

But they may no longer be measuring the right reality.

Perhaps becoming "data-driven" isn't the goal.

Perhaps becoming **hypothesis-aware** is.

The organizations that thrive are rarely those with the most dashboards.

They're the ones that continuously ask:

**"What assumptions are these dashboards making about the world?"**

That question may be more valuable than the numbers themselves.
