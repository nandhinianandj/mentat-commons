I had registered for this Chennai Marathon sponsored by Freshworks on last Sunday and couldn't go (or decided not to go(Rather heartbroken, I was really excited about going to the city i was born in and joke I didn't like the weather so I ran way when i was a 15-day old baby :-P) because of training interruptions and other issues). Since I'm also working on running regularly and love having running T-shirts, I wanted to atleast get the t-shirt and since a friend was going for the actual run I asked him to collect the bib and t-shirt for me. He called me on Saturday and said they gave him a form for me to fill, sign, take a picture and send for him to collect. He thought it was a some letter of statement/disclaimer.. I told him okay, get the file and send me.. I was house sitting for a friend and could not step out to go take a print out and come back..... So was wondering what could i do about it. 

He got the file sent it and called me, and I told him i can't take a print out and all.. May be will try to paste a digital image of my signature on the file(after filling out other details) and send it. He said may be you can write a letter and take a picture and send that. He also said the file looked weird. Here's the file that they sent,.If you look at it is a standard disclaimer for any event organizer who is organizing an event that has health risks and claiming that the organization is not liable for any physical harm. This is really standard practice in most running events, trekking events, or adventure sports events. Except I'm in Bengaluru and not physically able to participate in the run. Just bloody wanted the t-shirt. So I hand-wrote a letter saying, I'm not able to come and so just give this t-shirt to my friend instead(I also added my aadhar card digital copy since it's become a everywhere thing, great personal data privacy.. :-P) . They apparently were adamant i had to send that particular form. 

This back and forth has already had my friend spent already a 45-mins(estimate from first call/message timing, probably an hour on his actual time) in this back and forth. So I didn't want to stress him out or ask him to go fight for a t-shirt and decided to let it go. Now today, few days after that I'm planning a embodied participatory workshop(and so have been thinking from a L & D perspective), and coming up with a new activity, and instructions. The thought I was put in is the mind of processes, efficiency, effectiveness. And the aphorism of Efficiency is not the same as effectiveness. Now let's start with something, both are relevant only in the context of a goal. Without a goal it stops making sense to talk about either.

Efficiency once a goal has been established and agreed upon is often about time taken, Sometimes about how much input resources it takes.(But not always, if this resources--> goal equation is well-known/established. only time is the variable parameter/metric to measure/compare). Effectiveness is about what is called alignment in the current overton window(thanks to AI and other hype and critics around it). In the sense that effectiveness always has to be aligned with the global/bigger picture/objectives. I see the Vision-Mission difference is another similar construct, I've seen in some hospitals. Either way the point here is having one single form printed, and only few fields filled by hand-writing and signed is a process designed around and about how efficiently the form can be processed. Reminds me of this blog analyzing the US(originally BBC, but US did a re-make and revision) TV serial The office. 


The fact that whoever did the Bib collection point and organized didn't think or care about people who may not be able to come but want the t-shirt is a efficiency decision/choice(and a reasonable one at that I'd argue/defend). On the other hand, the person who saw the hand-written letter and decided to reject it, made some choice/decision that I can question. I can probably theorize about the reasons/story behind it, if I'm trying to guess. However I can point out this much, safely (and still a guess, but pretty confident it is right) that they failed to be effective and at being aligned to the event organizer's vision. (Which I'm guessing has some parts/elements of giving a smooth experience to all the participants, even ones who don't show up on the day, but registered before.). 


Either way, my focus on writing this piece is about pointing out how difficult is it to create a vision and way more importantly aligning everyone in the organization/team to both internalize it and also validate their decisions against it, whenever they make it. 

Different approaches exist to avoid this like for example: crowd-sourcing the vision itself. The climax scene of the Manirathnam movie Guru comes to mind as a cinematic attempt(even though it is more of a narrative and meaning-making attempt to paint history and future in a certain light for a specific preparation event, And in effect is more a symbolic collaborative vision creation than actual) to depict something like that.

What do I want y'all to take away? Well I hope I can leave the readers(who are executives at the endpoint) with a critical lens of comparing/measuring any decision they make against the alignment vs efficiency. And if the readers are managers/designers of an event/decision-tree I hope you take away a lens/reminder to think of how to convey the alignment to your executives when they have to decide something outside of the decision tree. 

Because as a random participant, I left with a bitter taste, disappointment(and actually questioning if I should try the Chennai Marathon again?) about Freshworks(I'm aware the actual event management partner might be a different organization altogether, but wouldn't apportion all the blame to them. Blame management is one reasons sometimes organizations decide to outsource things like Event management. ) However my curiosity(which is what I usually leave open after processing other emotions, after a conflict/incident like this ) that remains about this case is how did they fail in transmitting that alignment and at what level?? What was the history of transactions/collaborations between those two people that could've caused this gap/failure in alignment transmission? What kind of processes/activities can be done(think of it is team cohesion maintenance ) to prevent this type of failures??


P.S(self-meta note): They say once an IBM'er always an IBM'er referring to the obsession about process. I definitely rebelled against it in my career, but I guess I have always remained an IBM'er in that sense.
