> Trust isn’t a switch.
> [pause: 1 breath]
>
> It’s not yes or no.
> [pause: 1 breath]
>
> It’s a shape.
> [pause: 2 breaths]
>
> A vector.
> [pause: 1 breath]
>
> At least complex.
> [pause: 1 breath]
>
> Maybe quaternion—
> four components moving at once:
> [pause: 2 breaths]
>
> safety.
> honesty.
> repair.
> power.
> [pause: 3 breaths]
>
> I can trust your kindness
> and not your memory.
> [pause: 2 breaths]
>
> I can trust your intent
> and not your impact.
> [pause: 2 breaths]
>
> I can trust you with tears
> and not with my future.
> [pause: 3 breaths]
>
> But trust has too many dimensions
> for language to hold cleanly.
> [pause: 2 breaths]
>
> So we compress it.
> [pause: 1 breath]
>
> Into “loyal.”
> Into “not loyal.”
> [pause: 2 breaths]
>
> Into “good person.”
> Into “red flag.”
> [pause: 2 breaths]
>
> And the moment trust gets flattened—
> people bleed in silence.
> [pause: 4 breaths]
>
> Misaligned by one degree,
> the whole structure collapses.
> [pause: 3 breaths]
>
> That’s why crying becomes expensive.
> [pause: 2 breaths]
>
> Because the room demands
> a binary answer
> from a many-bodied truth.
> [pause: 6 breaths]
>
> And when people demand binary trust,
> they usually mean compliance.
> [pause: 5 breaths]
