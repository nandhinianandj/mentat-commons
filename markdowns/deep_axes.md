/axis-i — Systems & Decision Design

Designing data and organizational systems that support real decisions

The problem we address

Most organizations already collect large amounts of data.
What they lack is decision clarity.

Common symptoms:

Dashboards that don’t change behavior

Models that exist outside decision workflows

Metrics that reward activity rather than outcomes

Decisions that stall because ownership is unclear

Learning that doesn’t feed back into structure

Axis I exists to address these failures directly.

Our orientation

We treat decision-making as a system, not an individual skill.

A decision system includes:

information flows,

analytical models,

incentives and metrics,

authority and accountability,

and feedback from outcomes.

Improving one part in isolation rarely works.
Axis I engagements look at the system end-to-end.

How engagements typically begin

We start by identifying one or two high-impact decisions, not abstract goals.

Examples:

“Which projects should we fund next quarter?”

“When do we trust this model enough to act?”

“Who decides trade-offs when metrics conflict?”

We then examine:

what information is available at decision time,

who has authority to decide,

how uncertainty is handled,

and how outcomes are reviewed.

This diagnostic phase often reveals that the core issue is not technical.

Core Work Areas
1. Data, AI & ML Systems

Focus: systems that support judgment, not just prediction.

What we do

Design end-to-end data pipelines tied to specific decisions

Build ML models with explicit assumptions and limits

Integrate analytics into operational workflows

Design monitoring for drift, misuse, and failure modes

What makes this different

Models are evaluated by decision usefulness, not accuracy alone

Documentation explains why and when to use outputs

Teams are trained to maintain and adapt systems post-handoff

Typical outcomes

Shorter decision cycles

Reduced dependence on individual experts

Higher adoption of analytical systems

2. Decision Systems & Organizational Design

Focus: making decision processes explicit and improvable.

Many organizations rely on implicit decision rules.
This work makes those rules visible and adjustable.

What we do

Map how key decisions are actually made (not how they’re described)

Identify breakdowns in information, authority, or incentives

Redesign decision rights and escalation paths

Align metrics with real priorities

How this is done

Combines data analysis with structured conversations

Uses facilitation only where necessary

Focuses on decisions with material impact

Typical outcomes

Clearer ownership and accountability

Fewer stalled or circular discussions

Metrics that reflect real goals

3. Research & Framework Development

Focus: long-horizon thinking under uncertainty.

This work supports organizations where:

problems are ill-defined,

data is incomplete,

and outcomes emerge over time.

Current directions

Decision quality under uncertainty

Collaborative and inclusive decision-making

Consensus building and negotiated trade-offs

Learning systems inside institutions

How this is used

Strategy and policy design

Leadership development

Internal learning programs

Public or internal thought leadership

Engagement Models

Diagnostic (2–8 weeks)
Clarify decision bottlenecks and recommend focused interventions.

Delivery & Architecture (3–12 months)
Design and implement systems teams can own and evolve.

Embedded Advisory
Ongoing support during scale, transition, or restructuring.

What changes when this works

Organizations typically see:

faster alignment on priorities,

fewer decisions deferred due to ambiguity,

systems that improve through use,

and learning that feeds back into structure.

The goal is not optimization for its own sake, but better judgment over time.

/axis-ii — Learning, Story & Collaboration

Building organizational capability through practice

The problem we address

Many organizations invest heavily in training.
Very few see sustained changes in how people think or act.

Common issues:

Training focuses on content, not capability

Learning is disconnected from real work

Teams don’t practice decisions before they matter

Reflection is missing from delivery cycles

Axis II treats learning as organizational infrastructure, not an event.

Teaching and facilitation stance

All work here is grounded in two principles:

Constructivist: people learn by building and doing

Experiential: understanding emerges through practice and reflection

This applies whether the topic is data, collaboration, or leadership.

What sessions actually look like

Participants:

work on real problems from their context,

build artifacts rather than consume slides,

rehearse decisions under realistic constraints,

reflect on outcomes individually and collectively.

Sessions are structured, facilitated, and time-bound — not open-ended discussions.

Core Work Areas
1. Training & Capability Building

Focus: transferable skills, not tool familiarity.

Programs include:

Python, SQL, Data Science, and AI/ML

Systems thinking and decision literacy

Applied analytics for non-technical leaders

How learning is assessed

Project-based evaluation

Peer and facilitator feedback

Reflection on design choices and trade-offs

Learners leave with artifacts they can reuse and explain.

2. Facilitation & Collaboration Design

Focus: improving coordination under uncertainty.

This work supports teams facing:

cross-functional tension,

unclear ownership,

difficult trade-offs,

or rapid change.

What we do

Design workshops around real decisions

Surface assumptions and competing priorities

Support alignment without forcing consensus

Methods draw from adult learning, facilitation, and applied improvisation — always tied back to organizational goals.

3. Writing & Knowledge Translation

Focus: turning experience into shared understanding.

This includes:

internal essays and synthesis documents,

facilitation summaries that inform leadership,

talks and workshops that connect technical work to institutional context.

Writing is treated as a learning tool, not marketing output.

Outcomes organizations see

Stronger collaboration under uncertainty

Faster learning cycles

Increased confidence in decision-making

Training that results in changed behavior

How Axis II connects to systems work

Axis II ensures that:

systems designed in Axis I are actually used,

decisions are practiced before they carry risk,

learning feeds back into structure and metrics.

Together, the two axes support durable organizational intelligence.

How to start

Most engagements begin with a simple question:

Where does learning currently fail to change behavior?

From there, we design a focused intervention rather than a generic program.
