Training & Capability Building

Strengthening how teams think, decide, and learn together

Most organizational training is designed around goals:
adopt a tool, hit a metric, follow a framework.

That approach often fails.

Teams rush to solutions before understanding the problem, optimize for the wrong signals, and lose the ability to explain why a decision was made or how to improve it next time.

Mentat Commons training is designed differently.
It focuses first on process — how teams reason, coordinate, and learn — so goals can be pursued without collapsing complexity too early.

Two tensions we work with explicitly
Process vs Goal

Goals matter.
But when goals dominate too early, teams tend to:

hide uncertainty,

skip sense-making,

optimize appearances rather than outcomes.

This training helps teams:

slow down at the right moments,

make reasoning visible,

and build decision processes that continue to work even when goals change.

Goals become checkpoints — not starting points.

Data vs Story

Organizations often treat data and narrative as opposites:

data as objective,

story as subjective.

In reality, decisions always rely on both.

Data without story overwhelms and obscures assumptions.

Story without data drifts and resists correction.

This training teaches teams to move deliberately between data and story:

using data to test and refine narratives,

using narrative to decide what data matters and why.

How training is designed

Training sessions are built around sense-making loops, not content modules.

A typical loop includes:

A real situation from the team’s context

Available data or evidence

Competing interpretations and narratives

A decision or action

Reflection on assumptions and outcomes

Learning happens in the movement between these steps.

Two training streams
1. Decision & Data Practice

How teams reason with evidence, uncertainty, and trade-offs

This stream supports teams that:

use data but struggle to act on it,

defer decisions due to uncertainty,

or experience friction between technical and non-technical roles.

Participants practice:

asking better questions of data and models,

distinguishing signal from noise,

explaining decisions clearly to others,

revising conclusions when evidence changes.

The focus is on reasoning quality, not technical sophistication.

2. Collaboration & Sense-Making Practice

How teams coordinate and decide together under pressure

This stream supports teams facing:

cross-functional friction,

unspoken assumptions,

avoided disagreement,

or repeated coordination breakdowns.

Participants practice:

making decisions with incomplete information,

negotiating trade-offs without forced consensus,

clarifying decision ownership,

reflecting on how decisions were made — not just outcomes.

Methods draw from facilitation, theatre-based practice, and applied improvisation, always grounded in real organizational work.

Anonymized case vignettes
From “more data” to better decisions

Context
A mid-sized organization had dashboards across teams but frequent disagreement about what the numbers meant. Decisions stalled and were often deferred.

Intervention
We ran a short Decision & Data Practice lab focused on one recurring decision. Teams mapped how data was interpreted, where assumptions diverged, and how uncertainty was handled.

What changed

Teams stopped debating dashboards and started debating assumptions.

Decision ownership became explicit.

Follow-up decisions were faster, using the same data.

Key insight
The constraint wasn’t analytics — it was the absence of a shared reasoning process.

Practicing coordination before it failed

Context
A cross-functional leadership group experienced repeated friction during project handoffs. Issues surfaced late, often as conflict.

Intervention
We facilitated a Collaboration & Sense-Making session where the team rehearsed a high-stakes scenario using role-based simulation and structured reflection.

What changed

Unspoken expectations became visible.

Trade-offs were negotiated earlier.

Subsequent projects saw fewer last-minute escalations.

Key insight
The team didn’t lack alignment — they lacked practice deciding together.

How learning is assessed

There are no abstract exams.

Learning is assessed through:

tangible artifacts (decision maps, system sketches, narratives),

participant reflection,

facilitator observation,

application plans tied to upcoming work.

What matters is whether participants can:

explain how they reasoned,

justify why a path was chosen,

and revise thinking when conditions change.

How training connects to consulting

Training and consulting are designed to reinforce each other.

Consulting surfaces system and decision constraints

Training builds internal capability to address them

Learning feeds back into system design and metrics

Organizations may begin with training, consulting, or both.

How to start

Training engagements usually begin with a framing conversation around one question:

“Where are we rushing to goals before understanding our process?”

From there, we design a focused intervention rather than a generic program.
