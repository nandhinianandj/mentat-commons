AI / ML & Data Consulting

Designing data and machine learning systems that support real decisions

What problem this addresses

Many organizations invest in AI and analytics but struggle to see consistent impact.
Common reasons include:

unclear decision ownership,

models disconnected from real workflows,

fragile pipelines,

lack of trust in outputs,

and risks discovered too late.

This work focuses on designing AI and data systems as decision infrastructure, not standalone technology.

How I work

I start with decisions, not models.

Every engagement begins by clarifying:

which decisions matter most,

who is accountable for them,

what uncertainty is involved,

and what happens if the system is wrong.

Only then do we decide whether machine learning is appropriate — and how simple or complex the solution needs to be.

Core areas of work

Data Systems & Architecture

End-to-end pipelines tied to decision use, not just storage

Data ownership, validation, and observability

Models shaped around how information is actually consumed

Machine Learning Design

Modeling approaches chosen for context and risk

Assumptions and limits made explicit

Emphasis on interpretability and failure behavior

Production & Governance

Deployment pipelines with monitoring and rollback

Drift and performance decay detection

Governance practices that scale with use

Workflow Integration

Embedding outputs into existing tools and processes

Human-in-the-loop checkpoints

Clear guidance on when to trust, question, or override models

Typical engagement structure

Diagnostic (2–8 weeks)

Decision mapping

Data readiness assessment

Feasibility analysis

Clear recommendation (including when not to use ML)

Delivery & Architecture (3–12 months)

Incremental system build

Early validation of assumptions

Team training alongside delivery

Practical handover and documentation

Embedded Advisory

Ongoing support during scale or transition

Review of system behavior as conditions change

What makes this approach different

Decisions come before tools

Success is measured by decision usefulness, not accuracy alone

Failure modes are designed for upfront

Teams are enabled to own and adapt systems

Ethical and operational risks are treated as design constraints

This often leads to slower starts and far more durable outcomes.

Who this is for

Startups and scale-ups building core data systems

NGOs and public-interest organizations using data in sensitive contexts

Policy-adjacent and research teams transitioning prototypes to operations

Organizations that value judgment, accountability, and learning

What changes when this works

Organizations typically see:

faster and clearer decision cycles,

increased trust in analytical systems,

reduced dependence on individual experts,

systems that evolve rather than ossify.

The goal is not more automation, but better judgment over time.

How to start

We usually begin with one question:

“Which decision would benefit most from better information — and what happens if we get it wrong?”

From there, we determine whether AI, simpler analytics, or organizational redesign is the right next step.
